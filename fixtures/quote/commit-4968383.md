# Commit 4968383: 1.2.0: three checks whose evidence did not come from where it claimed

`nodejs/` reaches C-Produce, a gate ties the format's constants to the
document that defines them, and gate 7's blocked commands turn out never to
have been a decision about the book. Each was found the same way: by asking
where a check's evidence actually comes from.

nodejs/ at C-Produce
--------------------
§2.1 now has four independent derivations and §2.3 three witnesses beyond
the Rust. `blake3.js` is hand-rolled — a binding to the same C library the
Rust calls would test two callers of one implementation — and passes the
published vectors including the lengths straddling the chunk boundary.
`uid.js` reproduces all sixteen canonical encodings and uids, and implements
§7's shape clause with `uid()` running it first, so a malformed unit cannot
obtain an identity from the package.

The reading found four facts a C-Produce implementer cannot proceed without,
none of them in the spec, all recoverable only by decoding a fixture:

  - §2.2 said the OPPOSITE of what the encoder does. `deps` and `grounds`
    are listed "required, MAY be empty"; an empty one is omitted. A literal
    reading gives a five-key map where the reference emits three, which is a
    different uid for every unit with neither.
  - the status integers appeared nowhere, though rule M compares them as
    integers
  - the `source` map had no key layout, and `kind` was a second enum
  - the base32 alphabet was unnamed; base32hex was an equally faithful
    reading of the sentence

python/ and go/ had reached C-Produce through all four without recording
that they guessed. They got the same answers because they read the same
fixture, so nothing disagreed and nothing failed, for two releases.

make spec-tables
----------------
Parses the spec's tables rather than quoting them, and compares all four
implementations against them in both directions. 38 comparisons, its own CI
job. Until now every implementation read SMYSL_FORMAT_SPEC.md only to assert
it contained the string "Deterministic CBOR", while READINESS described the
§2.2 and §3.1 tables as "asserted against the document" — corrected here.

It found four more on its first run: the masthead read `smysl/0.1` while
§8.6 of the same document says `smysl/1.0` is what new documents declare;
and go/uid.go, python/smysl/uid.py and nodejs/src/uid.js each cited a
section the format spec has never had.

Eight breakages verified, each failing the right check.

Gate 7: 78 -> 85 transcripts
---------------------------
The 57 blocked commands were fixtures keyed by filename when a chapter's
state is keyed by position. Two of chapter 1's four states cannot be
committed under any arrangement — one is printed as a fragment, one as a
diff — so no fixture layout was ever going to reach them.

Chapters now replay as chapters: a scratch copy, walked in document order,
with `fmt --write` allowed to write. Later states are derived rather than
recorded. Reader edits live in edits.json, anchored by prose and never by
content, so the body is read out of the chapter at run time and nothing can
go stale. The scratch copy also removes a hazard that had never fired:
commands used to run in the fixture folder itself.

And it found real drift. beta.smy in chapter 29 is printed in full and its
transcript claims 14 records, 5 units; the document on the page has four,
and check says 12 records, 4 units. Seven transcripts depend on it,
including a uid the reader is told to type into `retract`. Held back rather
than repaired — the missing stanza cannot be reconstructed from the page.

Mutation figures sized
----------------------
Every survivor rate in gate 5 is the weak per-package claim. Measured on
store/mod.rs: 23 survivors per-package, 18 under --test-workspace. Five of
twenty-three were never gaps. The backlog is mostly real; this is one module
of one crate and applying the fraction to the other 357 would be the error
this file records twice already.

The first attempt was invalid and looked like a result: cargo-mutants took
its baseline at 1s of tests, auto-set a 20s timeout, then ran each mutant
against the 109-second workspace suite. `4 missed, 6 caught, 15 timeouts`
reads as nineteen of twenty-three being artefacts. --test-workspace needs an
explicit --timeout.
